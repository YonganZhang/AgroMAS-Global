<!-- id:title -->
# Coupling climate, watershed, market and satellite priors for wheat nitrogen management

<!-- id:sec-abs -->
## Abstract

<!-- id:abs-body -->
Synchronizing split nitrogen fertilization in global wheat production must sustain farm profitability and grain yield while keeping seasonal efficiency inside a safe bilateral corridor and curbing watershed nitrate surplus. Conventional process-based reinforcement learning and static split rules remain confined to stationary single-station fields with full in-situ sensors, leaving unresolved how coupled decision systems behave when climate warming, stringent nitrate caps, fertilizer price surges and unseen continental transfers act together. This work introduces AgroMAS-Global, a coupled five-specialist framework integrating warming-de-shifted climate analogues, watershed nitrate caps, market corridors and station-demeaned satellite priors with compact conformalized surrogates under non-anticipative three-stage planning verified by a mechanistic simulator. While remaining statistically equivalent to full-budget reinforcement learning under benign historical baselines (25.06 versus 27.00 percent compliance, 46 of 52 seasons tied, mean difference minus 1.94 percentage points, Holm p equals 1.00) and averaging 0.58 percentage points globally across all 180 factorial stress cells (40 wins, 93 ties and 47 losses), continuous bilateral coupling prevents soil-mining collapse (6.27-fold compliance gain over profit-only relaxation), breaks ahead by 5.15 and 5.61 percentage points along the operational 40 kg N ha$^{-1}$ ceiling under 1.8 and 3.5 degrees Celsius warming (7.19 to 8.10 percentage points under doubled prices), edges matched reinforcement learning on a seven-season Wageningen cohort (28.57 versus 19.05 percent), reduces serialized model storage over 1,690-fold versus 64-tree ensembles (166.3 KB versus 275.52 MB) alongside 38.5 percent higher compliance and up to 49.4 percent lower regret, and raises cross-continental prior gate admission from zero to 60.00 percent. These advances reveal how threshold-dependent coupling prevents both high-price humus depletion and cap-induced deadlocks.

## 1 Introduction

<!-- id:intro-p1 -->
Global cereal productivity doubled over the late twentieth century chiefly through synthetic nitrogen inputs, yet less than half of applied fertilizer nitrogen enters harvested grain worldwide [zhang2015]. Unrecovered nitrogen accelerates aquifer nitrate contamination, nitrous oxide emissions and coastal eutrophication, tightening the mid-century tension between food security and climate mitigation [lipper2014]. Wheat nitrogen management has therefore moved beyond yield maximization to co-regulating crop uptake, hydrological losses and soil organic nitrogen turnover across contrasting agroecosystems [chen2014]. Because unfertilized wheat still harvests nitrogen mineralized from native soil humus, a low seasonal surplus by itself never guarantees sustainable stewardship [vanderpol1992]. To prevent such disguised soil mining, the European Nitrogen Expert Panel instituted a safe bilateral corridor requiring apparent nitrogen use efficiency between 0.50 and 0.90 alongside nonnegative surplus below watershed limits [eunep2015].

<!-- id:intro-p2 -->
Prior investigations toward operationalizing seasonal nitrogen stewardship evolved along three distinct routes, each encountering critical friction outside stationary benchmark plots. Along the biophysical modeling route, dynamic process simulators resolve daily phenology, biomass partitioning and coupled soil carbon-nitrogen cycling [vandiepen1989]. Foundational field datasets calibrated early winter wheat nitrogen balance routines [groot1991]. Mature platforms such as WOFOST standardized water-limited cereal assimilate prediction [dewit2019]. Subsequent wheat phenology updates strengthened European cultivar timing accuracy [ceglar2019]. Recent soil-nitrogen couplings connected crop uptake directly with mineral and organic nitrogen pools to inform nitrate directives [berghuijs2024]. Parallel multi-crop platforms expanded management and soil representation across North American wheat belts [jones2003]. CropSyst quantified multi-year cropping sequence nutrient balances [stockle2003]. APSIM refined water-nitrogen interactions across drought-prone Mediterranean and subtropical systems [holzworth2014]. Regional yield-gap protocols bridged field simulations to strategic intensification assessments [vanittersum2013]. Spatial upscaling methods projected point diagnostics onto national production atlases [vanbussel2015]. Continental crop monitoring systems operationalized routine in-season forecasting across arable plains [vandervelde2019]. Along the agronomic stewardship route, multi-site trials matched split topdressing rates with crop developmental demand [cui2010]. Global syntheses established timing and placement rules improving cereal fertilizer recovery [fageria2005]. Chinese meta-analyses identified water-fertilizer thresholds optimizing joint productivity [yang2022]. Site-specific nutrient advisories raised nitrogen efficiency across intensive smallholder wheat plains [wang2020]. Sandy-soil field experiments quantified leaching abatement from reduced split regimes [faber2021]. Precision farming reviews cataloged canopy sensors and variable-rate application workflows [fountas2015]. Global indicator frameworks harmonized efficiency and surplus reporting across farming systems [norton2015]. Commercial farm surveys uncovered wide efficiency dispersion within intensive European arable zones [silva2021]. Recent monitoring across northwestern European arable rotations confirmed persistent room to trim excess inputs without yield loss [ravensbergen2024]. Regulatory syntheses linked uniform surplus caps to groundwater restoration targets [eu2016, fao2019, klages2020]. Long-term soil profiles further documented heavy deep nitrate accumulation under semi-humid plains [zhou2016] and sluggish organic carbon turnover across decades [huang2007]. Nevertheless, static split prescriptions falter whenever spring drought or heatwaves compress grain filling and alter soil mineralization [altenbach2003, iizumi2016]. Along the sequential optimization route, researchers linked simulators with digital twins and reinforcement learning to automate split timing [pylianidis2021]. Methodological reviews mapped sequential control opportunities under environmental stochasticity [gautron2022a]. Constrained multi-objective optimization established safety foundations for policy trade-offs [censor1977, roijers2013, achiam2017]. Initial gym wrappers turned crop models into reinforcement learning testbeds [overweg2021]. Subsequent environments added stochastic weather and rotation variability [gautron2022b]. Multi-year platforms like CyclesGym enabled soil nitrogen optimization across rotations [turchetta2022], while modular interfaces separated physical state evolution from noisy observations [maillard2023]. Deep reinforcement learning policies curbed inputs in stationary single-site climates [wu2022]. Imitation warm-starts accelerated policy discovery in agronomic action spaces [tao2022]. Watershed-coupled learners explored downstream nitrate load reductions [madondo2023]. Domain randomization countered transition drift across heterogeneous environments [tobin2017]. Reward design analyses warned that mis-weighted scalar objectives induce unintended shortcut exploitation [eschmann2021]. Safe reinforcement learning suites standardized constraint evaluation protocols [ji2023, sutton2018]. Notably, Baja and collaborators applied constrained reinforcement learning within a nitrogen-enabled wheat model to target bilateral efficiency at a single Dutch site [baja2025]. Harmonized soil grids supplied continuous physical property priors across unmeasured croplands [poggio2021]. In parallel, annual satellite foundation embeddings created a scalable spatial representation layer beyond ground sensors [brown2025, campsvalls2025].

<!-- id:intro-p3 -->
Despite these advances, current constrained controllers remain anchored to an isolated single-field formulation exhibiting four coupled structural gaps [baja2025]. First, instantaneous policies ignore post-decision weather uncertainty and anthropogenic warming trends, leaving controllers unable to balance impending leaching storms against late-season drought risk [asseng2015]. Second, uniform station assumptions overlook tight peri-urban watershed nitrate ceilings where strict hard filtering frequently empties candidate sets and triggers zero-dose deadlock [eu1991, klages2020]. Third, uncalibrated scalar rewards fail under input price shocks, allowing profit-seeking agents to boost short-term income by withholding nitrogen and mining soil humus [eschmann2021, tan2005]. Fourth, telemetry-dependent learners require expensive continuous field sensors, whereas feeding raw global satellite vectors into models conflates static geographic identity with seasonal dynamics [gaso2023, roberts2017]. This motivates two central scientific questions. How do continuous bilateral corridor retention and climate-adjusted forecast uncertainty govern the transition among deadlock, soil nitrogen mining and joint compliance across coupled climate, watershed and price stress. And can causal decomposition of static soil baselines from station-demeaned satellite anomalies enable reliable sensorless adaptation across contrasting continents without inflating predictive error.

<!-- id:intro-p4 -->
Closing these coupled gaps carries direct significance because fertilization failures cascade simultaneously across harvest stability, drinking-water compliance and farm solvency. Existing benchmarks focus predominantly on benign historical single-station years where simple heuristics already achieve comparable apparent performance, concealing acute controller fragility under compounding shocks [baja2025]. Without explicit bilateral corridor gradients, distributional uncertainty calibration and multi-region verification, advisory systems risk imposing zero-dose stalls in nitrate-vulnerable catchments or encouraging hidden organic nitrogen depletion under market stress [zhou2016, booth2023]. Equally important, naive green subsidies linked directly to uncalibrated model probabilities may elevate farm payouts without curbing watershed losses. Quantifying failure transitions across contrasting global archetypes therefore gives agronomists and catchment managers transparent operating boundaries prior to deployment [goldenits2024].

<!-- id:intro-p5 -->
To answer these questions, this study establishes AgroMAS-Global, a coupled five-specialist numerical architecture integrating spatial twin inference, de-shifted warming analogue forecasting, urban watershed safety screening, socio-economic corridor scoring and three-stage non-anticipative dynamic planning over an independent mechanistic crop-soil referee. Validation spans five global urban-breadbasket archetypes in Perth, Wichita, Lelystad, Beauce and Luancheng across historical baselines, temporal and cross-continental satellite holdouts, and a complete climate-cap-price-subsidy factorial surface. Three primary contributions advance coupled agricultural control. First, continuous bilateral corridor retention combined with non-anticipative continuation remains equivalent to reinforcement learning across mild historical years while preventing profit-driven soil-mining collapse (6.27-fold compliance gain), leading under 1.8 and 3.5 degrees Celsius warming and doubled fertilizer prices at the 40 kg N ha$^{-1}$ cap, and cutting serialized ensemble storage over 1,690-fold versus 64-tree baselines with 38.5 percent higher compliance. Second, conformalized zero-mean residual integration and station-demeaned satellite decomposition raise cross-continental prior gate admission from zero to 60.00 percent while transparently delineating out-of-region error bounds. Third, full factorial stress testing exposes cap-dependent warming crossovers, uncalibrated prior-subsidy misalignment and strict double-precision accounting verification across over six million daily transitions. Section 2 formulates the methodology, Section 3 analyzes comparative, ablation and spatial transfer experiments, Section 4 investigates factorial stress regimes and mass closure, and Sections 5 and 6 synthesize mechanisms and conclusions.

## 2 Materials and Methods

<!-- id:sec-m21 -->
### 2.1 Problem Formulation

<!-- id:m21-p1 -->
Seasonal wheat fertilization is posed as a finite-horizon non-anticipative control problem across three sequential phenological windows triggered when observable development stage crosses 0.20, 0.55 and 0.82 [ceglar2019, baja2025]. At each event $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, controllers choose discrete topdressing nitrogen $u_t \in \{0, 10, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100\}$ kg N ha$^{-1}$ subject to a cumulative seasonal ceiling of 220 kg N ha$^{-1}$, spanning 343 budget-legal action schedules. In the primary sensorless deployment track, real-time field telemetry of leaf area, soil water and plant nitrogen stays completely masked during online decisions.

<!-- id:m21-p2 -->
Total seasonal external input $I_N = \sum_{t=1}^{3} u_t + N_{\text{base}} + N_{\text{seed}} + N_{\text{dep}}$ accounts for topdressing, pre-sowing basal fertilizer, seed nitrogen and atmospheric wet deposition while excluding baseline soil organic nitrogen stocks, and grain removal is denoted $H_N$ [eunep2015]. Because $H_N$ incorporates nitrogen mineralized from native soil organic matter whereas $I_N$ falls as low as 3.5 kg N ha$^{-1}$ of seed and deposition when synthetic fertilizer is withheld, unfertilized soil mining mathematically pushes apparent efficiency $\text{NUE}_{\text{app}} = H_N / I_N$ well above 1.0 alongside negative surplus $S_N < 0$. Bilateral EUNEP sustainability therefore demands simultaneous satisfaction of $0.50 \le \text{NUE}_{\text{app}} \le 0.90$ and watershed-safe surplus $0 \le S_N \le S_{\text{cap}}$ alongside partial profit $\Pi$.

<!-- id:m21-eq1 -->
$$S_N = I_N - H_N, \qquad \text{NUE}_{\text{app}} = \frac{H_N}{I_N}, \qquad \Pi = p_g Y - p_f \sum_{t=1}^{3} u_t + \psi \mathbb{I}_{\text{joint}} - \tau \max(S_N, 0)$$

<!-- id:m21-p3 -->
where $Y$ is dry grain yield, $p_g = 200$ EUR per tonne is wheat price, $p_f = 1.5$ EUR per kg N scales baseline nitrogen cost by scenario multipliers, $\psi$ is the ecological compliance subsidy and $\tau$ denotes an optional nonnegative surplus levy fixed at zero in standard baseline evaluations. Independently, internal soil conservation monitors organic and mineral pool changes, daily bottom leaching and denitrification loss $L_{\text{nat}}$ and net mineralization to contrast apparent efficiency against native soil drawdown $\Delta N_{\text{org}}$ [silva2021, dewit2019].

<!-- id:sec-m22 -->
### 2.2 AgroMAS-Global Architecture

<!-- id:m22-intro -->
Collapsing competing meteorological, environmental and economic constraints into an unstructured scalar reward frequently induces shortcut depletion or constraint deadlock whenever stressors interact. AgroMAS-Global resolves this bottleneck by partitioning control over five typed numerical modules, namely the spatial twin, climate forecast, urban eco-safety, socio-economic policy and core agronomic specialists, communicating solely through public weather histories and calibrated probability distributions while an unchanged WOFOST81-SNOMIN engine acts as an isolated physical referee (Fig. 1). Thirty paired equivalence tests spanning 323 budget-legal schedules across both full-telemetry and sensorless tracks verify 100.0 percent decision agreement (zero action discrepancies) between this five-specialist coordinator and a consolidated monolithic solver, confirming that performance gains derive strictly from causal spatial adaptation, warming-de-shifted analogue branching, continuous bilateral corridor scoring and non-anticipative dynamic continuation.

<!-- id:sec-m221 -->
#### 2.2.1 Spatial Twin Specialist and Causal Satellite Prior Adaptation

<!-- id:m221-p1 -->
Online search across hundreds of budget-legal fertilization sequences precludes slow daily mechanistic loops and favors smooth calibrated surrogates running in sub-millisecond batches. The spatial twin specialist therefore replaces bulky tree ensembles with a compact three-seed multilayer perceptron mapping 36 normalized public features covering heat-unit progression, seasonal weather, cumulative doses and static soil properties through two 96-unit SiLU hidden layers (13,155 weights per network) to jointly estimate grain yield, grain nitrogen and executed doses with all weights frozen on 1995 to 2009 seasons. Because feeding raw satellite embeddings directly confounds fixed geographic coordinates with seasonal fertility shifts [brown2025], sensorless cross-regional transfer decouples hydraulic soil Ridge priors $\Delta_{\text{Static3}}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{soil}})$ capturing field capacity, wilting point and saturated conductivity from causal site-demeaned annual satellite anomalies $\Delta \mathbf{e}_{s,t-1}$ computed strictly up to prior year $t-1$.

<!-- id:m221-eq -->
$$\Delta \mathbf{e}_{s,t-1} = \mathbf{e}_{s,t-1} - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}_{s, \le t-1}|} \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}_{s, \le t-1}} \mathbf{e}_{s,y}, \qquad \hat{H}_N^{\text{adapt}} = \hat{H}_N^{\text{base}} + \Delta_{\text{Static3}}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{soil}}) + \mathbb{I}_{\text{in}}(\Delta \mathbf{e}_{s,t-1}) \Delta_{\text{PCA4}}(\Delta \mathbf{e}_{s,t-1})$$

<!-- id:m221-p2 -->
Whenever incoming anomalies exceed the weighted four-component trust region encoded by $\mathbb{I}_{\text{in}}$, out-of-distribution inputs fall back deterministically to $\Delta_{\text{Static3}}$ rather than extrapolating noise.

<!-- id:sec-m222 -->
#### 2.2.2 Climate Analogue Branching, Bilateral Corridor Scoring, and Dynamic Continuation

<!-- id:m222-p1 -->
To anticipate post-topdressing leaching storms and warming compression without peeking at realized weather, the climate specialist strips scenario warming offsets $\Delta T_{\text{scen}}$ from observed prefixes before matching historical weather analogues, thereby preserving daily rainfall clustering statistics while retaining full elevated temperature and carbon-dioxide covariates along physiological branches [asseng2015]. Every decision node reveals seven-day precipitation prior to temperature realizations so root doses obey strict non-anticipative information structure. Under tight nitrate ceilings where strict tail pruning empties feasibility sets, the urban eco-safety and socio-economic modules integrate a 2 kg N ha$^{-1}$ cap margin alongside a 1.5-weighted mining penalty ($\text{NUE}_{\text{app}} > 0.90$) across the full weather-by-residual product distribution, preventing agents from buying cheap surplus compliance via humus depletion [tessler2018, cawley2010].

<!-- id:m222-eq -->
$$J(u) = \mathbb{E}[\Pi(u)] + 250 \, P_{\text{active}}(\text{joint} \mid u) - 300 \, d_{\text{corr}}\!\left(\bar{S}_N(u), \bar{\text{NUE}}_{\text{app}}(u)\right)$$

<!-- id:m222-p2 -->
Here $P_{\text{active}}(\text{joint} \mid u)$ tracks expected joint corridor compliance over the active weather-by-residual distribution and $d_{\text{corr}}$ sums bilateral efficiency violations (penalizing $\bar{\text{NUE}}_{\text{app}} > 0.90$ at 1.5 times weight) alongside surplus violations evaluated on expected schedule outcomes $\bar{S}_N(u)$ and $\bar{\text{NUE}}_{\text{app}}(u)$. Notice that $\Pi(u)$ governs realized cash profit accounting (with external farm subsidy $\psi$), whereas the weighted terms in $J(u)$ act strictly as dimensionless corridor-alignment planning utilities. Finally, the core agronomic specialist solves three-stage non-anticipative continuation under a minimum 14-day inter-application interval, preserving the identical action-budget mask used by matched reinforcement learning baselines [ppo2017, mask2020].

<!-- id:sec-m23 -->
### 2.3 Conformal Surrogate Calibration and Spatiotemporal Validation Strategy

<!-- id:m23-p1 -->
Rather than approximating surrogate error through independent Gaussian variances that break coupling between yield and grain nitrogen uptake, out-of-fold errors are partitioned across five regions and three stages into 15 residual strata, sorted by surplus residual into 32 equiprobable quantile vectors and centered strictly to zero stratum mean to prevent systematic bias. Empirical farm profiles draw from their station-specific private residual bank while reverting to the equal-region global bank only for unseen synthetic soil keys, integrating every analogue weather branch across all 32 vectors during online planning. On the 2010 to 2014 development split, two-sided conformal calibration targets 90 percent empirical coverage and evaluates physical interval width alongside Brier skill across caps of 25, 35, 40 and 50 kg N ha$^{-1}$ and downstream profit ranking regret to guarantee probabilistic sharpness before closed-loop deployment [fisher1981, geurts2006].

<!-- id:m23-p2 -->
To prevent cross-regime leakage, validation separates 52 historical former-held-out seasons (2015 to 2026), 40 causal satellite-anomaly transfer years (2019 onward) and 7 independent Dutch Wageningen benchmark seasons into non-overlapping cohorts alongside inner-fold hyperparameter freezing for both leave-one-year-out and leave-one-region-out generalization [roberts2017, cawley2010]. All controllers run freshly from initial conditions inside unchanged WOFOST81-SNOMIN simulations, evaluated first within farm-year seeds and then across equal-region five-archetype averages using unified Holm-adjusted exact sign tests and paired bootstrap intervals [holm1979, phipson2010].

## 3 Experimental Results

<!-- id:sec-r31 -->
### 3.1 Dataset and Classic Case Description

<!-- id:r31-p1 -->
Across four continents, the five wheat archetypes segregate into physically distinct climatic warming trends, soil nitrogen endowment pools and dominant EUNEP compliance regimes rather than merely replicating homogeneous field baselines (Table 1 and Fig. 2a to c). As shown in Fig. 2a, historical climate forcing from 1980 to 2026 exhibits pronounced late-period temperature acceleration exceeding 1.5 degrees Celsius across Europe and Australia during the locked test horizon alongside steadily declining Dutch wet ammonium and nitrate deposition [asseng2015, clo2022]. Sequential chronological splitting (1985 to 1994 spin-up, 1995 to 2009 training, 2010 to 2014 conformal development and 2015 to 2026 testing) cleanly isolates non-stationary test extremes from surrogate fitting.

<!-- id:r31-p2 -->
Decomposing held-out seasons into five mutually exclusive EUNEP outcomes alongside regional agronomic indicators clarifies why a single static rule fails globally (Fig. 2b and c). Perth (17.4 t ha$^{-1}$ initial organic nitrogen) and Wichita (11.6 t ha$^{-1}$) occupy active fertilizer-responsive dryland corridors reaching 63.6 and 33.3 percent joint compliance, whereas maritime Lelystad achieves the highest grain yield (9.08 t ha$^{-1}$, 0.83 apparent efficiency) from modest organic pools (7.0 t ha$^{-1}$) yet hovers along the upper surplus ceiling (60.0 percent pure surplus exceedance, 20.0 percent compliance). In contrast, Beauce sustains heavy mineralization from fertile stocks (13.15 t ha$^{-1}$), pushing 91.7 percent of seasons above 0.90 apparent efficiency into soil nitrogen mining despite producing 8.53 t ha$^{-1}$. Conversely, Luancheng starts with lower organic reserves (5.64 t ha$^{-1}$) paired with heavy pre-sowing basal fertilizer plus deposition (174.50 kg N ha$^{-1}$ total input), whereas water-limited yields (3.43 t ha$^{-1}$) export only 69.79 kg N ha$^{-1}$ in grain, simultaneously depressing apparent efficiency to 0.40 (below the 0.50 floor) and stranding 104.71 kg N ha$^{-1}$ of excess surplus across 83.3 percent dual-failure seasons. Projecting regional 1-sigma covariance ellipses onto the bilateral input-removal plane (Fig. 2c) separates dryland compliance in Perth and Wichita, surplus-border concentration in Lelystad, upper soil mining in Beauce and lower-quadrant saturation in Luancheng.

### 3.2 Experimental Setup and Parameter Configuration

<!-- id:r32-setup -->
To test whether lightweight domain-coupled intelligence matches or surpasses heavy tree ensembles and on-policy reinforcement learning without real-time field sensors, AgroMAS-Global (Soft-Coupled) is evaluated against three controller paradigms sharing identical 220 kg N ha$^{-1}$ budgets and legal action masks. First, reference sequential and agronomic policies encompass a matched sensorless proximal policy optimization agent (20,616 trainable weights across actor and critic) and the full-telemetry agronomic split rule DVS-N2 [baja2025, ppo2017]. Second, surrogate baselines substitute 64-tree extremely randomized ensembles occupying 275.52 MB serialized storage per model (1,723,434 tree split nodes), weather-only networks and linear regression baselines for the compact three-seed twin neural ensemble (13,155 weights and 55.4 KB per checkpoint, totaling 166.3 KB), delivering a 1,696-fold serialized memory footprint reduction alongside 38.5 percent higher closed-loop compliance than tree ensembles, as well as 36.2 percent fewer trainable weights per network than the reinforcement learning policy (noting that neural parameters and tree split nodes are architectural counterparts rather than directly equivalent capacity metrics). Third, degraded planner variants isolate strict hard-tail pruning (Tier2-Composite), open-loop static scheduling, greedy single-step selection and profit-only bare relaxation. Statistical comparisons enforce identical seed averaging followed by full-family Holm-adjusted exact sign tests and equal-region bootstrap confidence intervals.

### 3.3 Comparative Experiments

<!-- id:r33-p1 -->
Across mild historical baselines, multi-specialist coupling matches full-budget reference controllers in overall frequency while preventing uncoupled soil-mining collapses and outperforming alternative surrogates and degraded planners (Table 2 and Fig. 3). Under these stationary historical conditions, sensorless Soft-Coupled control (25.06 percent) remains statistically indistinguishable from matched sensorless PPO (27.00 percent, paired difference minus 1.94 percentage points, 95 percent interval minus 8.55 to 4.09 percentage points, 46 of 52 farm-years tied, Holm p equals 1.00) and full-telemetry DVS-N2 (26.73 percent, 47 of 52 tied), confirming parity under benign stationary weather prior to stress differentiation. On the small-sample seven-season single-site Wageningen benchmark (where statistical power is inherently constrained by n equals 7), Soft-Coupled attains 28.57 percent compliance and 1457.25 EUR ha$^{-1}$ versus 19.05 percent and 1446.51 EUR ha$^{-1}$ for PPO and zero percent for DVS-N2. Crucially, compared with profit-only bare relaxation (4.00 percent), Soft-Coupled multiplies equal-region compliance 6.27-fold. While bare fallback, open-loop scheduling and weather-only control report mean apparent NUE values exceeding 1.0 (11.56, 1.68 and 1.31, Table 2), this occurs mathematically because EUNEP apparent NUE divides grain nitrogen harvested partly from mineralized native soil humus by minimal external inputs ($I_N$), producing inflated nominal partial profit (976.24 versus 887.77 EUR ha$^{-1}$ under bare fallback) strictly through unpriced soil nitrogen mining ($S_N$ minus 33.78 kg N ha$^{-1}$). Under identical sensorless inputs, Soft-Coupled also achieves relative compliance gains of 38.5 percent over ExtraTrees (18.09 percent), 39.8 percent over weather-only surrogates (17.94 percent), 73.4 percent over linear models (14.45 percent), 25.9 percent over open-loop schedules (19.91 percent) and 52.6 percent over greedy single-step policies (16.42 percent).

<!-- id:r33-p2 -->
Dissecting Fig. 3a to d reveals the exact statistical and sub-seasonal mechanisms driving these comparative rankings. In phase space (Fig. 3a), profit-only fallback shifts dramatically leftward into negative surplus (mean minus 33.78 kg N ha$^{-1}$) via unfertilized outliers along the vertical axis, whereas Soft-Coupled, PPO and DVS-N2 align their 1-sigma covariance ellipses along the central safety wedge. Nested policy bars (Fig. 3b) uncover why joint compliance clusters around 25 to 27 percent even for leading methods. Across ten controllers, most achieve 44 to 51 percent safe apparent efficiency and 52 to 58 percent satisfaction on at least one boundary, yet intersecting both bounds drops compliance sharply unless continuous corridor gradients rank borderline choices (giving Soft-Coupled a 16.1 percent relative lead over rigid Tier2-Composite at 21.58 percent and maintaining substantial distance above profit-only relaxation whose either-bound score stalls at 23 percent). On 349-day Western Australia dynamics (Fig. 3c), Soft-Coupled deploys a front-loaded decaying split schedule (100, 60 and 20 kg N ha$^{-1}$ across S1 to S3) immediately after early-season winter rainfall near day 30, rebuilding soil mineral nitrogen up to roughly 245 kg N ha$^{-1}$ to sustain tiller and stem-elongation uptake while avoiding excessive late-season leaching. Although back-loaded PPO and DVS-N2 (20, 80 and 80 kg N ha$^{-1}$) reach identical total executed nitrogen (180 kg N ha$^{-1}$) and final surplus (41.5 kg N ha$^{-1}$) with slightly lower cumulative seasonal loss (26.90 versus 31.45 kg N ha$^{-1}$), bare fallback applies zero doses throughout, draining mineral nitrogen nearly to exhaustion after S1 and forcing extreme mining (efficiency 40.50, surplus minus 138.2 kg N ha$^{-1}$). Across continents, Soft-Coupled translates these mechanics into distinct physical strategies, ranging from active full-season fertilization in Perth (177.3 kg N ha$^{-1}$) and Lelystad (220.0 kg N ha$^{-1}$) to cautious rainfed restraint in Wichita (77.5 kg N ha$^{-1}$) and unanimous zero topdressing above rich basal nitrogen (171.0 kg N ha$^{-1}$) in Luancheng. In Luancheng, 49.0 kg N ha$^{-1}$ of legal topdressing headroom (10, 20 and 40 kg N ha$^{-1}$ choices) remains unmasked by the 220 kg N ha$^{-1}$ cap, yet the controller withholds extra fertilizer because pre-sowing basal input and soil mineralization already saturate modeled grain nitrogen demand. Aligned bilateral rainclouds (Fig. 3d and Supplementary Figs. S4 to S5) corroborate that Soft-Coupled truncates both extreme mining outliers above 0.90 and excess surplus tails cleanly.

### 3.4 Ablation Experiments

<!-- id:r34-p1 -->
Single-module ablations distinguish primary safety guardrails whose removal collapses system compliance from finer predictive couplings that govern edge-regime refinements (Table 3 and Supplementary Figs. ## 5 Discussion

<!-- id:sec-d51 -->
### 5.1 Domain-Coupled Mechanisms and Surrogate Compression Efficiency

<!-- id:d51-p1 -->
This investigation demonstrates that encoding climate de-shifting, distributional quantile integration and bilateral corridor geometry inside collaborative specialists eliminates the need for oversized black-box networks or real-time field sensors. Integrating candidate schedules across complete weather-by-residual distributions rather than deterministic point predictions enables the eco-safety and economic specialists to weigh rare spring nitrate leaching storms directly against dryland organic nitrogen depletion across both semi-arid and maritime wheats. Equally important, decomposing external climate risks, watershed caps and bilateral incentives into explicit numerical operators liberates each surrogate neural network from memorizing non-stationary regime shifts through parameter inflation, allowing every ensemble member to maintain only 13,155 weights (55.4 KB per checkpoint and 166.3 KB across three members). Compared with 64-tree ensembles occupying 275.52 MB per serialized instance (1,723,434 decision nodes), this structured neural surrogate shrinks serialized deployment memory footprint over 1,690-fold while raising historical compliance by 38.5 percent relatively (25.06 versus 18.09 percent) and lowering calibrated profit ranking regret by 40.8 to 49.4 percent, while surpassing a 20,616-parameter reinforcement learning agent across warming and price-shock stressors along the standard 40 kg N ha$^{-1}$ ceiling with 36.2 percent fewer trainable weights per network. Because bilateral efficiency-surplus geometry and seasonal split staging characterize cereals and row crops broadly, embedding physical conservation inside compact structured planners offers direct methodological value across maize, rice and broader watershed agricultural systems.

<!-- id:sec-d52 -->
### 5.2 Evolution from Brittle Single-Field Pruning to Calibrated Regional Control

<!-- id:d52-p1 -->
Systematic divergence between strict tail pruning, soft corridor coupling and profit-only relaxation marks an essential conceptual shift for agricultural intelligence under compounding environmental stress. Under benign stationary seasons, conservative hard masks locate safe doses cleanly, whereas tightening nitrate directives or amplifying heat stress shrinks feasible envelopes toward zero and precipitates indiscriminate zero-topdressing stalls [baja2025]. Retaining continuous distance gradients resolves that conflict between safety conservatism and agronomic responsiveness across heterogeneous wheat belts while guaranteeing empirically verified zero-discrepancy equivalence (30 of 30 invariant tests across 323 plans) relative to monolithic solver architectures. At the public-policy interface, the prior-incentive misalignment exposed under uncalibrated subsidy rewards explains why agri-environmental payments in frameworks such as the Common Agricultural Policy must benchmark against conservative calibrated lower bounds rather than nominal model probabilities [klages2020]. In parallel, separating static physical soil baselines from causal station-demeaned satellite anomalies overcomes total cross-regional rejection by un-centered embeddings while lifting prior gate admission to 60.00 to 72.02 percent without claiming artificial predictive error drops [brown2025].

### 5.3 Mechanistic Boundaries, Trade-Offs, and Future Research Directions

<!-- id:d53-p1 -->
Important physical assumptions bound these findings and explain remaining cross-regime trade-offs. First, even compliant seasonal controls extract approximately 221 to 224 kg N ha$^{-1}$ from baseline soil organic nitrogen within an isolated reset wheat season, confirming that short-term apparent corridor compliance cannot substitute for multi-year crop-rotation stock stewardship. Second, lifting closed-loop cross-continental anomaly admission under frozen causal linear adapters leaves out-of-region grain nitrogen squared error marginally above static soil Ridge models (517.65 versus 505.15 kg$^2$ ha$^{-2}$), demonstrating that out-of-distribution gate passage does not automatically lower prediction error without regional fine-tuning. Simultaneously, 90 percent macro conformal coverage coexists with conditional undercoverage of 75.19 percent in semi-arid Western Australia, capping direct dryland deployment until region-stratified conformal recalibration is applied. Third, benchmark experiments rely on historical climate analogues, idealized warming shifts and complete annual prior-year satellite composites over representative regional profiles rather than operational synoptic weather forecasts, cloud-gapped near-real-time imagery, within-field textural transects or whole-farm machinery cost ledgers. Daily carbon-nitrogen balance verification confirms numerical accounting integrity within the simulator-controller pipeline rather than replacing empirical lysimeter observations. Finally, uncalibrated ecological subsidies tied directly to prior compliance probabilities risk incentivizing borderline surplus dosing without reducing deep nitrate leaching. Future work will pursue three concrete extensions, including within-region multi-texture soil and lysimeter groundwater nitrate monitoring transects, region-conditional conformal recalibration and subsidy coupling to calibrated lower bounds, and multi-season prospective field trials driven by operational numerical weather ensembles.
